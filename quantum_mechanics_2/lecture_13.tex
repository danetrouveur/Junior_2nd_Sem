\begin{thm}
(Russell-Saunders coupling scheme or LS coupling) The Russell-Saunders coupling scheme tells us that orbital angular momentum combines to give total $L$, spin angular momentum gives total $S$, and the total angular momentum of that configuration is given by the range $J=L+S,L+S-1,\ldots ,|L-S|$. 
\end{thm}
\vspace{2ex}
\begin{thm}
(Hund's rule) Hund's rule gives a approximation of the ground state of light atoms. The four steps are given as:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Maximise total spin quantum number $S$
\item[(i)] Maximise the total orbital angular momentum number $L$
\item[(iii)] If the subshell is less than half filled, the total angular momentum number $J$ is $|L-S|$ and if the subshell is more than half filled, the total angular momentum number $J$ is $|L+S|$. 
\item[(iv)] Use Pauli's principle in employing the steps above. 
\end{itemize}
\end{thm}

\begin{ex}
(Carbon $Z=6$) The electron configuration of carbon is given by the Aufbau principle as $(1s)^2(2s)^2(2p)^{2}$. Then,
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Maximising spin gives $S=1$.
\item[(ii)] Maximising orbital angular momentum gives $L=1$.
\item[(iii)] As the subshell is less than half filled, the total angular momentum is $J=|L-S|=0$.
\end{itemize}
In spectrascopic notation, 
\[{}^{3}P_{0}\]
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}
(Oxygen $Z=8$) The electron configuration of oxygen is given by the Aufbau principle as $(1s)^2(2s)^2(2p)^{4}$. Then,

\item[(i)] Maximising spin gives $S=1$.
\item[(ii)] Maximising orbital angular momentum gives $L=1$.
\item[(iii)] As the subshell is more than half filled, the total angular momentum is $J=|L+S|=2$.
\end{itemize}

\end{ex}

\begin{ex}
(Maganese $Z=25$) The electron configuration of manganese is given by $(1s)^2(2s)^2(2p)^{6}(3s)^2(3p)^{6}(4s)^2(4d)^{5}$. Then,
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Maximising spin gives $S=5/2$.
\item[(ii)] Maximising orbital angular momentum gives $L=0$.
\item[(iii)] As the subshell is exactly half filled, the total angular momentum is $J=S=5/2$.
\end{itemize}
In spectrascopic notation,
\[{}^{6}S_{5/2}\]
\end{ex}
