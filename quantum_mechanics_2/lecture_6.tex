\begin{lem}
(Relativistic kinetic energy) Recall that the relativistic energy kinetic energy was given as
\[E=\sqrt{(\vec{p}c)^2+(mc^2)^2}-mc^2\]
Taylor expanding, we find
\[E=\dfrac{\vec{p}\,^2}{2m_{e}}-\dfrac{1}{8}\dfrac{(\vec{p}\,^2)^2}{m^3_{e}c^2}+\ldots \]
and we will use
\[H_1=-\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\dfrac{\vec{p}\,^2}{2m_{e}}\Big)^2\dfrac{1}{m_{e}c^2}\]
Finding the first order pertubation for this potential, we find
\[\langle \phi _{nlm}|H_1|\phi _{nlm}\rangle =-\dfrac{1}{2}m_{e}c^2(Z\alpha )^2\Big[\dfrac{2(Z\alpha )^2}{n^3(2l+1)}-\dfrac{3(Z\alpha )^2}{4n^{4}}\Big]\]

\end{lem}

\begin{lem}
(Spin-orbit coupling) The spin of the electron and its associated magnetic moment 
\[\vec{\mu }=-\dfrac{e}{2m_{e}}g\vec{S}=-\dfrac{e}{m_{e}}\vec{S}\]
change the interaction of the electron with the electric field that is produced by the proton charge. An electron moving with a uniform velocity $\vec{v}$ experiences a magnetic field
\[\vec{B}=-\dfrac{\vec{v}\times \vec{E}}{c^2}=-\dfrac{\vec{v}\times \vec{E}}{c^2}=-\dfrac{\vec{v}\times (-\nabla \phi (r))}{c^2}=\dfrac{1}{c^2}\vec{v}\times \dfrac{\vec{r}}{r}\dfrac{d \phi (r)}{d r} \]
from here, the additional interaction becomes
\[H_{2}=-\vec{\mu }\cdot \vec{B}=-\dfrac{e}{m_{e}}\vec{S}\cdot \vec{v}\times \dfrac{\vec{r}}{rc^2}\dfrac{d \phi (r)}{d r}=-\dfrac{e}{m_{e}^2c^2}\vec{S}\cdot \vec{L}\,\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{d \phi (r)}{d r}  \]
Taking $\phi (r)=Ze/(4\pi \varepsilon_0 r)$, we find
\[H_{2}=\dfrac{Ze^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0}\dfrac{1}{m^2_{e}c^2}\dfrac{\vec{S}\cdot \vec{L}}{r^3}\]
where a relativistic calculation adds another factor of $1/2$. We conclude
\[H_{2}=\dfrac{Ze^2}{4\pi \varepsilon _{0}}\dfrac{1}{2m_{e}^2c^2}\dfrac{\vec{S}\cdot \vec{L}}{r^3}\]

\end{lem}
