\begin{prop}
The real hydrogen atom is only truely solved when dealing with the Dirac equation and the potential
\[\dfrac{-e^2}{r}\]
The spin-orbit interaction is given by
\[
\[\begin{cases}
\vec{E}'=\gamma (\vec{E}+\vec{\beta }\times \vec{B})-\dfrac{\gamma ^2}{\gamma +1}\vec{\beta }(\vec{\beta }\cdot \vec{E})\\
\vec{B}'=\gamma (\vec{B}-\vec{\beta }\times \vec{E})
\end{cases}\]
In the electron's rest frame, the lab frame variables $(\vec{E},\vec{B})$ becomes $(\vec{E}',\vec{B}')$. Here,
\[\vec{\beta }=\vec{v}/c\qquad\&\qquad \gamma =\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta ^2}}\]
Take $\vec{B}=0$, we have 
\[\begin{cases}
\vec{E}'=\vec{E}\\
\vec{B}'=-\vec{\beta }\times \vec{E}=-\dfrac{\vec{v}}{c^2}\times \vec{E}=-\dfrac{\vec{v}}{c^2}\times (-\nabla \phi )=\dfrac{\vec{v}}{c^2}\times \hat{r}\,\dfrac{\partial \phi }{\partial r} 
\end{cases}\]
We have
\[H_{SO}=-\vec{\mu }_{e}\cdot \vec{B}'\]

\end{prop}
