\begin{ex}
Consider the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}={\bm C}^2$ of a qubit system. An arbitrary mixed state is expressed as
\[\rho =\sum ^{2}_{i=1}w_{i}|\phi_{i}\rangle \langle \phi _{i}|\]
Consider
\[\begin{cases}
	|\phi _{1}\rangle =|x;\uparrow\rangle =\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}
	1\\ 1
	\end{pmatrix}\\
	|\phi_2\rangle =|z;\uparrow\rangle =\begin{pmatrix}
	1\\0
	\end{pmatrix}
\end{cases}\]
with $w_1=2/5$ and $w_2=3/5$. The density matrix is then given as
\[\begin{align*}
	\rho =&\dfrac{2}{5}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}
	1\\1
	\end{pmatrix}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1\ 1)+\dfrac{3}{5}\begin{pmatrix}
		1\\0
	\end{pmatrix}(1\ 0)\\
	=&\dfrac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}
		4&1\\1&1
	\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}\]
Calculating $\rho ^2$ we find that $\rho ^2\ne \rho $ and that $\rho $ is a mixed state. It is a positive operator as
\[\rho -\rho ^2=\dfrac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}
	3&0\\0&3
\end{pmatrix}
\]
We can lastly preform a spectral decomposition with $p_{1,2}=1/2\pm \sqrt{13}/10$ and
\[\phi =p_1|1\rangle \langle 1|+p_2|2\rangle \langle 2|=\rho \]	
Other examples are cooling and optical. 
\end{ex}

\begin{defi}
(Pertubation theory) There are very few quantum systems that we can solve analytically. Pertubation theory provides a way to approximate quantum results and is very important. Let's first look at the big picture. An arbitrary Hamiltonian can be written as
\[\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda \hat{V}(t)\]
where we fully know $\hat{H}_{0}$ as in the eigenvalues, eigenkets and etcetera. We should also take $\lambda $ to be very small for this to make sense. There are large two classes, (1) time independent pertubations and (2) time dependent pertubations. Each are defined as pertubations where $\hat{V}$ is independent and dependent of time. Let's deal with time independent pertubation theory for now.
\end{defi}

\begin{ex}
(Non-degenerate pertubation) Given the information of $\hat{H}_{0}$ we want to inquire the following,
	\[E_{n}^{(0)}\quad\&\quad |n^{(0)}\rangle\qquad\implies \qquad |n\rangle _{\lambda }\quad\&\quad E_{n} \]
 where $\hat{H}|n\rangle _{\lambda }=E_{n}|n\rangle _{\lambda }$. We automatically expect 
\[E_{n}= E_{n}^{(0)}+\mathrm{\Delta }_{n}(\lambda )\]
and thus
\[(\hat{H}_{0}-E_{n}^{(0)})|n\rangle _{\lambda }=(\mathrm{\Delta }_{n}(\lambda )-\lambda \hat{V})|n\rangle _{\lambda }\]
As $H_{0}|n^{(0)}\rangle =E_{n}^{(0)}|n^{(0)}\rangle $, $\langle n^{(0)}|(H_{0}-E_{n}^{(0)})=0$ which leads to
\[\langle n^{(0)}|\mathrm{\Delta }_{n}(\lambda )-\lambda \hat{V}|n\rangle _{\lambda }=0\]
Telling us that $|n^{(0)}\rangle $ is missing from the ket of the right-hand side. Now define the projection from
\[{\bf 1}=\sum _{k}|k^{(0)}\rangle \langle k^{(0)}|\]
where the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ has the basis $\{|k^{(0)}\rangle  \}$ as
\[\phi _{n}={\bf 1}-|n^{(0)}\rangle \langle n^{(0)}|=\sum _{k\ne n}|k^{(0)}\rangle \langle k^{(0)}|\]
givens some $n$. Now,
	\[\boxed{|n\rangle _{\lambda }=C_{n}(\lambda )|n^{(0)}\rangle +\dfrac{1}{E_{n}^{(0)}-\hat{H}_{0}}\phi _{n}(-\mathrm{\Delta  }_{n}(\lambda )+\lambda \hat{V})|n\rangle _{\lambda }}\]
Notice that $\langle n^{(0)}|n\rangle _{\lambda }=C_{n}(\lambda )$ and that $C_{n}(\lambda )$ dictates normalisation (how much $|n^{(0)}\rangle $ is in $|n\rangle _{\lambda }$). We expect the following
	\[|n\rangle _{\lambda }=C_{n}(\lambda )\Big(|n^{(0)}\rangle +\lambda |n^{(1)}\rangle +\lambda ^2|n^{(2)}\rangle +\ldots \Big)\]
now we of course want to substitute. 
\end{ex}
