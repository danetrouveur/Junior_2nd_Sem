\begin{defi}
(Non-degenerate pertubation energies) We would now want to identify the higher orders of the energy, no the eigenvectors. We can applying $\langle n^{(0)}|$ to the expansion and repeating the process above, we obtain, for first order,
\[\begin{align*}
E_{n}^{(1)}=&\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(0)}|n^{(1)}\rangle +\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle \\
=&\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle 
\end{align*}\]
and for the second order,
\[\begin{align*}
E^{(2)}_{n}=&\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(0)}|n^{(2)}\rangle +\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(1)}\rangle \\
=&\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(1)}\rangle \\
=&\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}\dfrac{1}{E^{(0)}-H^{(0)}}\phi _{n}(H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle )\\
=&\sum _{k\ne n}\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}\dfrac{1}{E^{(0)}-H^{(0)}}|k^{(0)}\rangle \langle k^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle \\
=&\sum _{k\ne n}\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|k^{(0)}\rangle \dfrac{1}{E^{(0)}-E_{k}^{(0)}}\langle k^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle \\
=&\sum _{k\ne n}\dfrac{|\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(1)}|k^{(0)}\rangle|^2 }{E^{(0)}_{n}-E_{k}^{(0)}}
\end{align*}\]
Notice how we have considered $\langle n^{(0)}|H^{(0)}|n^{(i)}\rangle =0$. This comes from the previous fact that higher orders came with a projection operator that elimiates the $|n^{(0)}\rangle $ in them. 

\bigskip

I think we've done enough work worthy of memorising! The first and second Taylor coefficients for energy are essentially the expectation value of the perturbing potential and the weighted sum of coalescing states.
\end{defi}
\vspace{2ex}

\begin{ex}
(Simple harmonic oscillator) Consider the solved harmonic oscillator with basis vectors $|n^{(0)}\rangle $ and $E^{(0)}_{n}=\hbar \omega (n+1/2)$. Applying a uniform electric field, $Ex$,
\[\lambda H^{(1)}=(-e)(-Ex)=eEx\]
For the first order, we have
\[\lambda E_{n}^{(1)}=\langle n^{(0)}|\lambda H^{(1)}|n^{(0)}\rangle =\langle n^{(0)}|eE\sqrt{\dfrac{m\omega }{2\hbar }}(a+a^{\dagger })|n^{(0)}\rangle =0\]
For the second order, we have
\[\lambda ^2E_{n}^{(2)}=\sum _{k\ne n}\dfrac{1}{E_{n}^{(0)}-E_{k}^{(0)}}{|\langle n^{(0)}|eE\sqrt{\dfrac{m\omega }{2\hbar }}(a+a^{\dagger })|k^{(0)}\rangle |^2}=-\dfrac{(eE)^2}{2m\omega ^2}\;<\;0\]
We know that this result, however, is exact. This is because of the form of the Hamilonian,
\[H^{(0)}+\lambda H^{(1)}=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}+\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega ^2x^2+eEx=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}+\dfrac{m\omega ^2}{2}(x-K)-\dfrac{(eE)^2}{2m\omega ^2}\]
where $K$ is a constant. Note that in the ground state,
\[\lambda^2 E_{0}^{(2)}=\sum _{k\ne n}\dfrac{|\langle n^{(0)}|\lambda H^{(1)}|k^{(0)}\rangle |^2}{E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{k}}\;<\;0\]

\end{ex}
