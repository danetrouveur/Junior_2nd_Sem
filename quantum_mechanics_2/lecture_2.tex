\begin{recall}
We have learned that the expectation value of a certain observable was given by
	\[\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\hat{A})\]
Using the spectral decomposition $\hat{A}=\sum a|a\rangle \langle a|=\sum a\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}$, we can express the probability of observing $\hat{A}$ and obtaining $a$ as 
	\[\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})\]
You should check how this works for a pure state? Notice that
\[\dfrac{\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}}{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a})}=\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{\mathop{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}}\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})=1\]
and that the trace is 1.
\end{recall}

We check that the density matrix satisfies
\begin{align*}
\dot{\rho} 
&= \frac{d}{dt} \big( |\psi\rangle \langle \psi| \big) \\[6pt]

&= -\tfrac{i}{\hbar} [ H, \rho ]
\end{align*}
\end{ex}

\begin{rmk}
(General properies of $\hat{\rho }$) Notice that the square of the density matrix is equal to
\[\hat{\rho }^2=\sum _{j}p_{j}^2|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
Therefore, in general, we say mixed states are states whose density matrix satisfies
\[\hat{\rho }\ne \hat{\rho }^2\]
and a pure state otherwise. Also,
	\[\hat{\rho }-\hat{\rho }^2=\sum _{j}(p_{j}-p_{j}^2)|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
and the expected value is always bigger than 0. Sometimes, we write $\hat{\rho }\geq \hat{\rho }^2$. We also ask, what is the norm of $|\hat{\rho }\psi \rangle $ for an arbitrary $|\psi \rangle $? We notice that
\[\langle \hat{\rho }\psi |\hat{\rho }\psi \rangle =\langle \psi |\hat{\rho }^2|\psi \rangle =\langle \psi|\rho \psi \rangle \leq  \sqrt{\langle \psi |\psi \rangle }\sqrt{\langle \hat{\rho } \psi |\hat{\rho } \psi\rangle } \]
Resulting in
\[\langle \hat{\rho }\psi |\hat{\rho }\psi \rangle\leq \langle \psi |\psi \rangle  \]
We see that the density matrix is a contraction mapping. We now prove the contraposition -- if $\hat{\rho }^2=\hat{\rho }$, the state is pure. Last class we have seen that the eigenvalue of the matrix becomes either 1 or 0. In the spectral decomposition,
\[\hat{\rho }=\sum _{j}p_{j}|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
we see that only one of the $p_{j}$'s should become one and that the others should be zero. We have now concluded the proof. 
\end{rmk}

\begin{thm}
(Relationship between diagonal and non-diagonal density matrices) Always, you want to find the diagonal form of an operator. How do you find it from a mixed state?
\[\hat{\rho }=\sum ^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}|\psi _{i}\rangle \langle \psi _{i}|\longleftrightarrow \hat{\rho }=\sum ^{m}_{i=1}w_{j}|\alpha _{j}\rangle \langle \alpha _{j}|\]
We obviously know that $n\leq m$. Take
\[\langle \psi _{k}|\hat{\rho }|\psi _{k}\rangle =0\]
Then,
\[\sum ^{N}_{j=1}w_{j}|\langle \psi _{i}|\alpha _{j}\rangle |^2=0\]
which tells us that
\[\langle \psi _{i}|\alpha  _{j}\rangle =0\]
Therefore,
\[|\alpha _{j}\rangle =\sum ^{n}_{k=1}\langle \psi _{k}|\alpha _{j}\rangle |\psi _{k}\rangle \]
We now define a $m\times n$ matrix
\[V_{ij}=\sqrt{\dfrac{w_{i}}{p_{j}}}\langle \psi _{j}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \]
It is obvious that
\[\sum ^{n}_{j=1}V_{ij}\sqrt{p_{j}}|\psi _{j}\rangle =\sqrt{w_{i}}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \]
and we have
	\[\sum ^{m}_{i=1}(V^{\dagger})_{ji}V_{ij}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{p_{j}p_{k}}}\sum ^{m}_{i=1}w_{i}\langle \psi _{j}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \langle \alpha _{i}|\psi _{k}\rangle =\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{p_{j}p_{k}}}\langle \psi _{i}|\rho ^2|\psi _{k}\rangle =\delta _{jk}\]

\end{thm}
