\begin{thm}
(Born rule in the language of density operators) Using the spectral decomposition of a certain state ${A}=\sum a|a\rangle \langle a|=\sum a\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}$, we can express the probability of observing ${A}$ and obtaining $a$ as 
\[\boxed{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}({\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})}\]
What would happen in the case of a pure state?
\[\dfrac{\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a}}{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}_{a})}=\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{\mathop{Tr}}(\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}}\hat{\rho }\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}_{a}})=1\]
and that the trace is 1.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}
(Time evolution of the density operator) We check that the density matrix satisfies, for a pure state,
\[\begin{align*}
\dfrac{d \rho }{d t}=& \dfrac{d }{d t}\Big(|\psi (t)\rangle \langle \psi (t)|\Big)=\Big(\dfrac{d }{d t}|\psi (t)\rangle  \Big)\langle \psi (t)|+|\psi (t)\rangle \Big(\dfrac{d }{d t}\langle \psi (t)| \Big)\\
=&-\dfrac{i}{\hbar }H|\psi \rangle \langle \psi |+\dfrac{i}{\hbar }|\psi \rangle \langle \psi |H=-\dfrac{i}{\hbar }[H,\rho ]
\end{align*}\]
We that, therefore, the ``objects that move" is the state.
\end{thm}

\begin{rmk}
(Classification of ${\rho }$) Notice that the square of the density matrix is equal to
\[\hat{\rho }^2=\sum _{j}p_{j}^2|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
Therefore, in general, we say mixed states are states whose density matrix satisfies
\[\hat{\rho }\ne \hat{\rho }^2\]
and a pure state otherwise.
\end{rmk}
\vspace{2ex}
\begin{proof}
Let's compare $\rho ^2$ and $\rho $
	\[{\rho }-{\rho }^2=\sum _{j}(p_{j}-p_{j}^2)|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
and the expected value is always bigger than 0. Sometimes, we write ${\rho }\geq {\rho }^2$. We also ask, what is the norm of $|{\rho }\psi \rangle $ for an arbitrary $|\psi \rangle $? We notice that
\[\langle{\rho }\psi |{\rho }\psi \rangle =\langle \psi |{\rho }^2|\psi \rangle =\langle \psi|\rho \psi \rangle \leq  \sqrt{\langle \psi |\psi \rangle }\sqrt{\langle {\rho } \psi |{\rho } \psi\rangle } \]
Resulting in
\[\langle {\rho }\psi |{\rho }\psi \rangle\leq \langle \psi |\psi \rangle  \]
We see that the density matrix is a contraction mapping. We now prove the contraposition -- if ${\rho }^2={\rho }$, the state is pure. Last class we have seen that the eigenvalue of the matrix becomes either 1 or 0. In the spectral decomposition,
\[{\rho }=\sum _{j}p_{j}|\psi _{j}\rangle \langle \psi _{j}|\]
we see that only one of the $p_{j}$'s should become one and that the others should be zero. We have now concluded the proof. 
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}
(Alternative decompositions) Let's say that we have a certain mixed state. How do we find the relationship between a mixed state expressed in an arbitrary basis and a basis that is not necessarily orthogonal? Let say you have a orthogonal basis $\{|\psi _{i}\rangle \}$ where $n$ is the number of eigenketes with non-zero eigenvalues. How does it map onto another representation?
\[{\rho }=\sum ^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}|\psi _{i}\rangle \langle \psi _{i}|\qquad \mapsto \qquad {\rho }=\sum ^{m}_{j=1}q_{j}|\varphi  _{j}\rangle \langle \varphi  _{j}|\]
We obviously know that $n\leq m$. Take
\[\langle \psi _{k}|\hat{\rho }|\psi _{k}\rangle =0\]
Then,
\[\sum ^{N}_{j=1}w_{j}|\langle \psi _{i}|\alpha _{j}\rangle |^2=0\]
which tells us that
\[\langle \psi _{i}|\alpha  _{j}\rangle =0\]
Therefore,
\[|\alpha _{j}\rangle =\sum ^{n}_{k=1}\langle \psi _{k}|\alpha _{j}\rangle |\psi _{k}\rangle \]
We now define a $m\times n$ matrix
\[V_{ij}=\sqrt{\dfrac{w_{i}}{p_{j}}}\langle \psi _{j}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \]
It is obvious that
\[\sum ^{n}_{j=1}V_{ij}\sqrt{p_{j}}|\psi _{j}\rangle =\sqrt{w_{i}}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \]
and we have
	\[\sum ^{m}_{i=1}(V^{\dagger})_{ji}V_{ij}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{p_{j}p_{k}}}\sum ^{m}_{i=1}w_{i}\langle \psi _{j}|\alpha _{i}\rangle \langle \alpha _{i}|\psi _{k}\rangle =\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{p_{j}p_{k}}}\langle \psi _{i}|\rho ^2|\psi _{k}\rangle =\delta _{jk}\]

\end{thm}
