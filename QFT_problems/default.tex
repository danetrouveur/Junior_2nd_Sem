\begin{ex}
Lets write $n^{i}=p^{i}/|p|$. Recalling that for both representations, the Lorentz generators can be written as $M^{ij}=\epsilon^{ijk}\sigma ^{k}/2$:
\[\begin{align*}
H=&S_{i}n^{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}M^{jk}n^{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon ^{jkl}\sigma ^{l}\Big)n^{i}=\dfrac{1}{4}(2\delta ^{l}_{i}\sigma ^{l})n^{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sigma _{i}n^{i}
\end{align*}\]
In vector notation, we can write $\vec{\sigma }\cdot \vec{p}/2$ where the components of $\vec{\sigma }$ are the Pauli matrices.

(b) Recalling that the Pauli matrices are orthogonal and idempotent, we have
\[H^2=\dfrac{1}{4}(\sin ^2\theta \cos ^2\phi\, \sigma_1^2+\sin ^2\theta \sin ^2\phi\, \sigma_2^2+\cos ^2\theta \,\sigma_3^2)=\dfrac{1}{4}(\sin ^2\theta \cos ^2\phi +\sin ^2\theta \sin ^2\phi +\cos ^2\theta )=\dfrac{1}{4}\]
Therefore, the eigenvalues of $H$ are $\pm \frac{1}{2}$. Writing the matrix as
\[H=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}
\cos \theta &e^{-i\phi }\sin \theta \\
e^{i\phi }\sin \theta &-\cos \theta 
\end{pmatrix}
\]
We can find the vectors with eigenvalues $+1/2$ and $-1/2$ as
\[\xi  ^{+}(\vec{p})=\begin{pmatrix}
\cos (\theta /2)\\
e^{i\phi }\sin (\theta /2)
\end{pmatrix}\qquad\&\qquad
 \xi ^{-}(\vec{p})\begin{pmatrix}
-e^{-i\phi }\sin (\theta /2)\\
\cos (\theta /2)
\end{pmatrix}

\]

\end{ex}
